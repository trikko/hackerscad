Objects and types

Solid
A 3D body: boxes, cylinders, booleans, features. Every operation gives a new one.
Profile
A flat 2D outline on XY (rect, circle, text, sketch.profile; a shape's profile is a one-shape sketch with everything given): combine with + - &, extrude or revolve it into a Solid.
Path
A curve in space (path, spline, helix) to sweep a profile along.
Surface
Faces with an edge open, no inside (a curve swept, a loft through paths): split a solid with it, thicken it, sew surfaces into a solid.
Vec
A 2D or 3D vector or point: vec(10.mm, 5.mm, 0.mm); .x .y .z .length.
Vec2
A 2D vector.
Vec3
A 3D vector.
Direction
An axis direction: X, Y, Z, -X…, or any vector.
Plane
An infinite plane: an origin and a normal (XY, plane(…), body.plane(faces.top)); cuts, mirrors, sections.
Axis
A line in space: an origin and a direction (axis(…), body.axis(edges…)); rotations and circular patterns.
Frame
A coordinate system on a face or plane: origin, X, Y and Z (body.frame(faces.top)); placeOn builds on it.
BBox
A solid's bounding box: min, max, size, center.
Halves
What halves gives: .above (where the plane's normal points) and .below.
Edges
A selection of a solid's edges, for fillet and chamfer: edges.parallel(Z), edges.of(faces.top)…
Faces
A selection of a solid's faces, for shell, draft, frames: faces.top, faces.normal(X)…
Edge
One edge, inside a selector predicate: edges.filter(e => e.isCircle && e.radius == 3.mm).
Face
One face, inside a selector predicate: faces.filter(f => f.center.z > 10.mm).
Sketch
A constrained 2D drawing: points, lines, arcs, circles, ellipses and constraints; the solver places them, .profile gives the outline.
Drawing
A technical sheet (views, sections, dimensions) returned by a @drawing function; exported as PDF, SVG, DXF.
Assembly
Parts placed by their connections (planned).
Color
A colour: "steelblue", "#RRGGBB", "#RRGGBBAA" or rgb(…).
Length
A length with its unit: 10.mm, 2.5.cm; bare numbers given to geometry are mm.
Angle
An angle: 30.deg, 0.5.rad.
Area
An area (Length × Length).
Volume
A volume.
bool
true or false.
int
An integer.
double
A number.
float
A number.
real
A number.
long
An integer.
string
Text.

rect(w: Length, h: Length, p: Vec, q: Vec, center: Vec, angle: Angle, corner: Length) → Profile
A rectangle: centred on the origin (or center:), from two opposite corners, or from one corner and its size; angle: turns it about its centre (a rotated, tilted rectangle, at an angle), corner: rounds its corners. s.rect with everything given.

A circle centred on the origin, or round a point. s.circle with everything given.

An ellipse centred on the origin (or round a point), rx along X, ry along Y; angle: turns it. s.ellipse with everything given.
polygon(points: Vec[], smooth: bool, n: int, r | d: Length, fit: string, angle: Angle, center: Vec) → Profile
A polygon through the points (smooth: true, a smooth closed curve through them instead, the same as a sketch's closed spline: whichever point it starts at); or a regular one of n sides, centred on the origin or round a point: d/r the circle through its corners, or with fit: "sides" the circle its sides touch; angle: the direction of its first corner, else its first side lies at the bottom, horizontal. s.polygon with everything given.
slot(length: Length, w: Length, p: Vec, q: Vec, center: Vec, angle: Angle) → Profile
A stadium: length end to end along X, centred on the origin (or center:, angle: turned); or round two centres p and q, w wide. s.slot with everything given.
teardrop(r | d: Length, angle: Angle, flat: bool, center: Vec) → Profile
A hole that prints sideways without supports: a circle with a 45° point towards +Y (up on a wall); angle: another point (3° to 90° from the vertical), flat: true cuts the point. s.teardrop with everything given.
s.knurl(face: Faces, kind: string, pitch: Length, depth: Length, angle: Angle, margin: Length) → Solid
A grip raised depth (0.5 mm) on a face of any shape (a knob's side, a handle, a flat pad, a lofted grip), laid on it as wrap: does: kind "diamond" (the default), "straight" ridges along the face, or "diagonal" ones leaning at angle (30°, also the diamonds' slant); pitch (1.6 mm), round a face that closes on itself (a cylinder, a loft through rings) adjusted so a whole number go round and the pattern closes; margin (0.5 mm) left plain along the face's edges. Put it last: a boolean after it meets every cell.
p.tiles(kind: string, size: Length, gap: Length, round: Length, angle: Angle, tile: Profile | Profile[], step: Vec[2], origin: Vec, whole: bool, scale: number | (Vec2 centre) => number, keep: (Vec2 centre) => bool, seed: int, jitter: number, relax: int, points: Vec[]) → Profile
The region filled with cells, gap apart (each shrunk by gap/2), cut at its border (whole: true keeps only the cells wholly inside): subtract it for a grille, cut or extrude it on a face for a pattern of pockets or reliefs (taper: slants their sides). kind: "square", "hex", "triangle", "brick", "octagon" (with small squares), "diamond", size the distance between neighbouring cells' centres; "voronoi", cells round points about size apart, jitter: 0 (hexagons) to 1 (the default), relax: smoothing passes for evener cells, seed:, or points: your own. Or your own tile: one profile or several (a sketch's), repeated by step: [a, b]; the copies must not overlap, an error says which do. round: rounds each cell, angle: turns the tiling, origin: where a cell sits (the region's centre); scale: a factor for each cell, keep: which cells stay, both functions of its centre. With no gap (the default) the cells touch: they are joined into one area. A region drawn on a face or lying in another plane (a section) is tiled there, in that plane's coordinates, and its tiles stay there.
text(text: string, size: Length, font: string, weight: string | int, bold: bool, italic: bool, spacing: Length, lineSpacing: number) → Profile
A text outline starting at the origin on the baseline (lines below one another, the last on it); font: an installed font's name, "Sans", "Serif", "Monospace" or a family (a warning, naming similar ones, when it is not installed and another is used), a style at its end counting ("Sans Bold Italic"), or a font file's path; weight: "thin", "extralight", "light", "regular", "medium", "semibold", "bold", "extrabold", "black" or 100 … 900 (one the font has not installed: the nearest, with a warning), bold: true for "bold", italic: true; spacing: 1.mm sets the letters that much further apart (negative: closer), lineSpacing: 1.5 the lines 1.5 times as far as the font has them. A narrower or wider font is a family of its own: "DejaVu Sans Condensed".
p.round(r: Length) → Profile
Rounds every corner, outward and inward, holes too; an error names a corner r is too large for.
p.offset(distance: Length) → Profile
Grows (d > 0) or shrinks the outline (shrunk to nothing: an empty profile, isEmpty); on a plane, the parallel plane.
p.spots(n: int, margin: Length) → Vec3[]
Up to n points inside the profile (in any plane), at least margin from its edges, as far apart as they can be: where to put keys, pins or feet; one point: the middle; none when nothing is that far from the edges.
p.across(axis: X | Y) → Profile
The profile stood up across X or Y, as extrude(along:) stands it (across X: its x along Y, its y up Z), at the origin: move it along the axis and loft through several, or extrude it where it is.
s.offset(distance: Length, sharp: bool) → Solid
Grows (d > 0) or shrinks the solid, round where it turns (its walls thickened); sharp: true its faces moved and extended until they meet, edges staying sharp. Holes it grows over close up (filled).
p.extrude(height: Length, taper: Angle, symmetric: bool, twist: Angle, along: X | Y, shear: Vec) → Solid
A solid along +Z from the profile (a negative height: down, towards -Z; not zero); taper: 3.deg leans its sides in going away from the profile, whichever way the solid goes (the outline narrows, holes widen; a negative taper widens); twist: 90.deg turns it around Z as it rises, evenly; shear: vec(dx, dy) moves its top that much sideways (in the profile's own x and y) from its bottom, the sides leaning straight: an oblique prism (a blade's bevel, a leaning slot). symmetric: true puts half the height each side of the profile, which is then the middle of the solid (a taper narrows both ways from it, a twist turns half each way). along: X stands the profile up across X (its x along Y, its y up Z) and pushes it towards +X, along: Y (its x along X) towards +Y; a negative height towards -X or -Y. A profile lying in another plane (a section, one turned or drawn on a face) is extruded across its own plane, from where it is.
p.revolve(axis: Axis, angle: Angle) → Solid
Turns the profile around an axis in its plane, on one side of it (touching it at most), by an angle up to 360°; without one, x is the radius and y the height along Z. A profile drawn on a face or plane, or lying in one (a section), turns where it lies: its axis a world one (X, Y, Z through the plane's origin, or an Axis anywhere), as everything else in that plane; without one, round its own y axis (one drawn on XZ: round Z), stood up when its y is not upright.
p.sweep(path: Path, frenet: bool, twist: Angle | (t) => Angle, up: Direction, guide: Path) → Solid
Moves the profile along a path, across it (a curve, a Path on XY, instead: a Surface); how it turns about the path: as little as it can, or one of frenet: true (with the curvature: helices), twist: 360.deg (once, evenly; t => angle: by that much at t, 0 at the start to 1 at the end), up: Z (its +Y towards that way all along; an error where the path runs that way), guide: rail (its +Y towards the point as far along that path).
loft(profiles: Profile[], ruled: bool, inside: Vec3[][]) → Solid
A solid through profiles placed in space; through paths (Path[]), a Surface; or through rings of points (Vec3[][], the same count each): one smooth surface closed round them, much faster for many rings; inside: rings for an inner wall, hollow with a floor, open between the last rings (which lie in one plane).
hull(shapes…) → Solid
The smallest convex shape around solids and profiles: hull(a, b) or hull([a, b]); profiles in one plane give a profile, lying there (one of no place taken into it); in different planes, a solid. Their flat faces it lies on keep their tags. grow: 2.mm makes it that much bigger all round, rounded (2% farther at most, never nearer): quicker and surer than an offset of a hull.
minkowski(a, b) → Solid
a moved by every point of b: minkowski(box(…), sphere(2.mm)) rounds the box; one of the two must be convex. Their flat faces, moved out, keep their tags; a's own tags and references stay.
s.cut(profile: Profile | Solid, on: Frame | Faces | Plane, at: Vec3, depth: Length, through: bool, taper: Angle, wrap: Faces, project: Faces, along: Vec3) → Solid
Digs a profile into a face: body.cut(p, on: frame, depth: 2.mm); body.extrude(p, on: …, depth:) raises it the same way. On a curved face (one face of any shape): wrap: rolls the profile onto it like a label, its lengths kept along the face (exact on cylinders, cones and extrusions, a little stretched on a sphere), its x across the face (round a cylinder) and y along it, read from outside; project: carries it onto the face along a direction (along:, else the face's normal), from the plane touching the face, as a light casts it (stretched where the face turns away). The profile's origin goes to the front of the face (the side facing -Y, halfway along), or to at: a point over it; depth goes along the face's normal. depth is always positive: cut digs in, extrude raises (a negative depth is an error: use the other one).
s.thread(face: Faces, size: string, pitch: Length, length: Length, turns: number, starts: int, left: bool, clearance: Length, chamfer: bool, flip: bool, taper: Angle) → Solid
A thread on a cylindrical face, outside a shaft or inside a hole (made at the tap drill size): "M8", "M8x1", "1/4-20 UNC", "#10-32 UNF", "Tr12x3", "G1/2", tapered "R1/2" and "1/2 NPT"; taper: 1.79.deg makes any thread conical (each side's lean, narrowing to the free end).
s.extrude(faces: Selector, depth: Length, along: Vec3) → Solid
Pulls flat faces of the solid outwards: body.extrude(faces.top, depth: 5.mm); along: slants the sweep; body.cut(faces.top, depth: 2.mm) pushes them in.

Tags

s.addTag(tag: string | string[], recursive: bool) → Solid
Tags the solid (a profile, a path, a surface) as an object: b.addTag("body"). A tag is a name, not blank and without ';'; one with a dot belongs to the module named before its last dot ("hinges.pin"), given only there. recursive: true tags each of its faces as well, which keep it when the solid is joined to or cut from another. Changes the variable it is called on and gives the solid back, for a chain.
s.addTag(tag: string | string[], at: Vec3 | Axis | Plane | Frame) → Solid
Tags a point, an axis, a plane or a frame on the solid: it moves, turns, mirrors and scales with it, and stays through cuts and joins (a copy joined to itself holds it once). Split (split, halves, pieces), each piece has the references lying in it or on it, one on the cut both, one in none (a hole's axis) the nearest; an intersection drops those in what it cuts away; a pattern keeps its first copy's; minkowski(a, b) a's. Given again, the tag's new reference replaces the old. Read it back with b.point(tag), b.axis(tag), b.plane(tag), b.frame(tag).
s.point(tag: string) → Vec3
Where the reference tagged so is now (a point, or the origin of an axis, plane or frame): b.point("pin").
s.axis(tag: string) → Axis
The axis tagged so (also a plane's normal or a frame's Z through its origin); a circle or arc tagged in the sketch: its centre, across the sketch.
s.plane(tag: string) → Plane
The plane tagged so (also an axis's or a frame's, across it).
s.frame(tag: string) → Frame
The frame tagged so, or one on the point, axis or plane tagged so (its X as plane(…).frame() has it): to place a part there with placeOn.

struct Joint(a: Solid, b: Solid, loose: Solid[], print: Solid[])
The two pieces of a solid cut along a plane with a joint between them, and the loose parts that lock it (a wedge), if any.
splitJoint(body: Solid, at: Plane, kind: string = "tenon", size: Length = …, length: Length = …, clearance: Length = …, turn: Angle = …, count: int = 2, keys: int = …) → Joint
`body` cut along the plane `at` into two pieces that fit together with a joint, `clearance` apart where they meet (0.2 mm suits most FDM printers: see the fits guide). The joint is centred on the cut (the middle of the section), laid along the section's longer side; `turn` turns it further about the plane's normal; `size` sets its scale (by default the square root of the section's area: the width of a square beam) and `length` how far it reaches across the plane (0.6 of the size). `count` (or `keys`, as in splitFlat and mold) is the number of fingers, pins or dowels (pins thinner the more there are). The fingers share the section's width; pins and dowels go in a row across its middle or, where that is hollow (a tube), on the material round it, as thick as it has room for; a tenon there is an error. - "tenon": a tenon on a, its mortise in b (hozo); - "dovetail": a tail across the piece, sliding in sideways (ari); - "pins": round pins on a, holes in b; - "fingers": a box joint, fingers of a and b in turn; - "lap": a half lap (ai-gaki), each piece keeping half the thickness; - "kama": a gooseneck, dropping in from the side (kama tsugi); - "kanawa": a half lap with hooks at both ends, locked by a wedge driven through the middle (kanawa tsugi): the wedge is in `loose`; - "koshikake": a dovetail over a seat that the other piece rests on (koshikake ari tsugi), which carries a load without bending open; - "barbed": a tenon with barbs, split down its middle, driven in: it goes in and does not come out. - "dowels": `count` loose dowels, the printed kind of wooden ones: a rod with two flats, each end split, half into a round hole of a and half into one of b, held by friction (the split halves press on the walls); they print lying on a flat (`print`), and the holes are plain round ones.
struct FlatSplit(a: Solid, b: Solid, keys: Solid[], aPrint: Solid, bPrint: Solid, print: Solid[])
The two halves of a solid cut flat along a plane, each to print lying on its cut face, and the flat keys that join them.
splitFlat(body: Solid, at: Plane, keys: int = 2, kind: string = "bowtie", size: Length = …, depth: Length = …, clearance: Length = …, wall: Length = …, count: int = …) → FlatSplit
`body` cut flat along the plane `at` into two halves, each printed lying on its cut face (nothing stands out of it, so it lies flat on the bed), joined by `keys` flat keys (or `count`, as in splitJoint): a pocket in each half's cut face, half the key in each. The keys print lying flat too, and fit their pockets `clearance` apart; glue them in, or leave them dry to take apart. The keys go where the cut has room for them, at least `wall` from its edges, as far apart as they can be. "bowtie" keys (butterflies, chigiri) keep the halves from sliding and from turning, even one alone; "disc" keys are round, two at least. `size` is a key's length (from the section's size unless given), `depth` how deep it goes into each half. The pockets open with a 0.4 mm chamfer: the first layers spread a little on the bed, and the pocket's mouth is on the bed.
joinParts(a: Solid, b: Solid, at: Plane, kind: string = "tenon", size: Length = …, length: Length = …, clearance: Length = …, turn: Angle = …, count: int = 2, keys: int = …) → Joint
Two separate parts joined where one meets the other: `b` ends on the plane `at` (a face of `a`, its normal pointing into `b`), and the joint goes from `b` into `a`: a shelf into an upright, a rail into a post. The joint is centred on `b`'s end and sized by it, as splitJoint does; what reaches past `a`'s far side stops flush with it (a through tenon). Kinds: "tenon", "dovetail" (a sliding dovetail, its housing running out of `a` the way it slides in), "pins", "fingers" (a corner of a box), "lap", "kama" (its housing too runs out of `a`), "koshikake" (a sliding dovetail over a seat, its housing running out of `a` too), "barbed" (driven in for good), "dowels" (holes in both, the dowels in `.loose` and, lying flat, in `.print`); not "kanawa", which joins two ends: splitJoint. `count` (or `keys`) as in splitJoint. Gives the two parts changed: `.a` with the hollows, `.b` with what fits in them.
jointKinds: string[]

struct Mold(a: Solid, b: Solid, aPrint: Solid, bPrint: Solid)
The two halves of a mold, where they go and laid for printing. Tags: the faces of the hollow the object leaves are "molds.cavity", the halves' faces along the parting plane "molds.parting", the pour hole's "molds.sprue".
mold(object: Solid, at: Plane, wall: Length = …, keys: int = 4, sprue: Length = …, vents: int = 2, seal: Length = …, clearance: Length = …, pry: bool = true, count: int = …) → Mold
A two-part mold to cast `object` in (silicone, resin, plaster): a block `wall` thick round it, cut along the parting plane `at`, the object's shape hollow inside. The plane should cross the object where it is widest, so each half lets go of the cast. - `keys` (or `count`, as in splitJoint) cones on a's parting face and their sockets in b's line the halves up (where the face has room, as far apart as they can be), `clearance` apart; - a seal round the cavity (its convex hull), a ridge `seal` high on a and its groove in b, keeps the liquid from creeping out between the halves (0.mm: none); - a pour hole `sprue` wide with a funnel, and `vents` thin channels for the air, go up through b from the cavity's highest points (along the plane's normal: put that side up when pouring). - `pry`: a notch on two opposite sides, across the parting line, to open the mold with a flat screwdriver without marring its faces. Both halves print with the parting face up, on their outer faces (`aPrint`, `bPrint`): the keys and the seal stand up, the sockets and the groove open upwards, with no supports.

import flex;

kerf(body: Solid, zone: Solid, width: Length = …, bridge: Length = …, pitch: Length = …, length: Length = …, along: Vec3 = …) → Solid
`body` made to bend where it lies inside `zone` (any solid: a box across a plate, a ring round a tube): rows of slots through its thickness, each row shifted half a slot from the next, so what is left is a lattice of thin bars that twist and bend (kerf bending, as in laser-cut plywood). The slots go through the part's thinnest way inside the zone, and run across the body's length (along its shorter side), so it bends along its length. `along:` (X, Y or Z) sets the way they run instead. The slots run along the axes X, Y and Z only, and go through along one of them: make a part that lies turned at an angle bend before turning it. - `width`: how wide each slot is (0.8 mm: two lines of a 0.4 mm nozzle leave it open); - `bridge`: the material between two slots of a row: the narrower, the softer it bends; - `pitch`: from one row to the next: the closer, the tighter it bends; - `length`: of each slot (six times the pitch unless given, shorter on a narrow part: its width less a bridge, so there is material at its ends). Slots that cut the part apart are an error. In PLA a few degrees per row; PETG and TPU bend much further. Print the part lying flat, the slots upright.
